  The empirical null is only defined for 3144 of the 10351 loci. A locus whose prevalence and phylogenetic signal place it in a bin holding too few others, or in one where near-identical clade markers collapse the spread to nothing, has no usable null and is left untested. The 7207 loci in that position are not null results: they were not tested.

  Under a correctly calibrated null the median p-value is 0.5 and the fraction below 0.05 is 0.05.
  The simulated null fails that test badly: median p 0.276 and 23.7 percent of patterns below 0.05, roughly five times the expected rate. The reason is specific and worth stating. Simulated traits are built from eight principal coordinates, and a real accessory locus confined to one clade has phylogenetic structure far sharper than eight axes can describe. The share of carriage those axes explain therefore understates how lineage-confined a k-mer really is, and the matched null is too weak. That same null was adequate in script 21, where it was applied to 33 defense systems whose distributions are much less clade-restricted than a typical accessory k-mer.
  The empirical null does not assume a model for that structure. It takes the spread of statistics among k-mers of the same prevalence and the same measured phylogenetic signal as the null, estimated by median and scaled median absolute deviation so that a few genuine associations cannot inflate it. Only its p-values are interpreted below.

WHAT THIS DESIGN CAN AND CANNOT SEE
  It can see a locus present in a reasonable number of strains whose presence tracks resistance beyond what its phylogenetic distribution predicts. That is the standard target of a bacterial association study and it is tested here without a catalogue.
  It cannot see a locus that is confined to one lineage, because the matched null deliberately removes exactly that signal and no observational design can separate a lineage-confined cause from the lineage. It cannot see point mutations, since presence of a k-mer is a presence or absence test. It cannot see anything that is not in an assembly.
