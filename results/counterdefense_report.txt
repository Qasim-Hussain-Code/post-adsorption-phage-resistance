  The restriction-modification family is the one place where a consistent pattern appears. Of the 5 RM subtypes tested against Anti_RM prevalence, 4 have an interaction odds ratio above 1, meaning RM protects less well against lineages whose relatives commonly carry anti-RM genes. RM_Type_IV reaches significance (interaction odds ratio 1.63, q = 0.015) and RM_Type_III and RM_Type_I point the same way without reaching it. Anti-RM proteins such as Ocr and ArdA are the best characterised and most widespread anti-defense proteins known, so this is the pairing where a lineage-level prior would be expected to carry the most information. The pattern is suggestive and it rests on one significant test out of 13; it is not established by this analysis.

  Among pairs in the subset 'gate passed, lacks lineage-validated defense', 75.8 percent of infections still fail, against 79.2 percent across the whole matrix. Applying the surface gate and removing hosts that carry a lineage-validated defense system barely moves the failure rate.
  The subset 'gate passed, lacks all 33 tested systems' is empty: every one of the 402 strains carries at least one of the 33 testable defense systems, so there is no defense-free comparison group to be had. MazEF alone is present in 338 strains. This is a real constraint on the design and not an oversight: a question of the form 'how often does infection fail in a host with no defenses' cannot be answered in this collection.
  This is the central quantitative result of the counter-defense analysis: the great majority of failed infections in this collection are not attributable to the surface determinants typed here, nor to the intracellular defense systems DefenseFinder detects, nor to anti-defense content imputed from phage lineage.

WHAT COULD NOT BE TESTED
  Resident prophages were not annotated. Superinfection exclusion by a resident prophage is a well-documented route to post-adsorption resistance and is a plausible contributor to the residual failure above, but identifying prophages would require running a prophage caller over 402 bacterial genomes, and those genomes are not part of the released data used here. This is a gap in the analysis rather than a question the data answer negatively.
  Per-phage anti-defense annotation was not performed, for the reasons given at the top of this report. Annotating the 96 genomes directly would sharpen the rescue test considerably and is the single change most likely to alter its conclusion.
  Receptor binding protein sequences were not compared. The released table names the receptor binding protein of each phage but does not give its sequence, and the phage genome files carry nucleotides without the gene coordinates needed to extract it, so grouping phages by receptor binding protein similarity was not possible.
