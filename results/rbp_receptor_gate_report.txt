INTERPRETATION
  Best receptor-based gate: similarity threshold 0.65 on the LPS outer core, 43 receptor groups, enrichment 1.26 fold against 1.34 fold for the genus gate.
  Grouping by receptor binding protein homology does not improve the gate. Since the receptor binding protein is the right biological object, this points at the anchor rather than the grouping: a single isolation host per phage can show that one surface state is permissive but never that any other is not, so the permissive sets stay small and the gate stays blunt however the phages are grouped.
  Under the best receptor-based gate, 60.6 percent of failed infections are attributed to surface exclusion, against 35.9 percent under the genus gate. That rise must not be read as support for the surface-masking account. The receptor-based gate attributes more failures to the surface only because it is stricter, admitting 40.9 percent of pairs against 65.7 percent, while its enrichment is lower. A gate that excludes more pairs and separates them less well is discarding signal along with noise, so the larger gate 1 share is an artefact of stringency rather than a better measurement. The share of failures genuinely attributable to the surface is not identified by either gate.
  The O-antigen status gates are worse than useless: several have an enrichment below 1, meaning pairs passing the gate are lysed less often than those failing it. Anchoring on a two-level determinant whose permissive state is almost always the common one produces a gate that tracks the rarity of O-negative strains rather than receptor compatibility.

  This is the strongest test of the two-gate framing this dataset allows without new experiments, because it uses the actual determinant of adsorption rather than a taxonomic stand-in. The conclusion of script 02 is unchanged: receptor compatibility in this collection cannot be reduced to a reliable binary call, and conditioning on surface state by stratification remains the defensible approach.
