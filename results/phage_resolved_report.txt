THE QUESTION
  Every association tested elsewhere in this project is an average over 94 phages. Defense systems are characteristically narrow in spectrum, so a system blocking a handful of phages would be diluted by that average into something no test here would see. If that is what has happened, the central negative result is an artifact of aggregation. This script tests each system against each phage separately and asks whether the averages have been hiding anything.

TWO NULLS, BECAUSE ONE IS NOT ENOUGH
  A null that matches prevalence and the share of carriage explained by the principal coordinates is not adequate here, and the reason is measurable rather than theoretical. Defense systems sit in islands inherited by near-identical relatives. Taking the fraction of same-cluster strain pairs that agree on carriage, the real systems sit at a median of 0.9111 while coordinate-matched null traits reach 0.8103, and 25 of the 33 systems exceed their own null on that measure. Since lysis is itself clonally structured, a trait that tracks lineages more tightly than the null does will score against it for reasons that have nothing to do with defense.
  The second null fixes exactly that. It takes the number of carriers in each clonal cluster and permutes those counts among clusters of the same size, so prevalence and the entire within-cluster carriage pattern are preserved while the lineages carrying the trait are randomised. Its median concordance is 0.9111 against the observed 0.9111.
  Neither null dominates. The coordinate null matches broad-scale structure and under-matches clonal structure; the clonal null does the reverse, carrying about half the broad-scale signal of the real traits. Every p-value reported below is therefore the worse of the two, so a system is credited only where neither null accounts for it.

PHAGE GROUPS, NOT PHAGES
  The panel of 94 phages contains near-identical members, with some pairs at zero distance. At a patristic distance of 0.1 it collapses to 54 groups. A system associated with one member of a group is associated with the rest for free, so counting phages would let a single lineage look like a broad spectrum. Distinct groups are counted below, which is the treatment given to clonal strains and to identical k-mer presence patterns elsewhere in this project.

  DOES IT REST ON ONE GROUP OF RELATIVES?
    Retron_I_C: dropping each of the 12 clonal clusters holding a carrier in turn leaves 1 to 12 phage groups (worst p 0.1329 across the 12 refits).
    Dropping cluster 164, which holds 6 of the carriers, takes it to p 0.1329.
    Losing that many strains costs power whatever they are, so the same drop was repeated 40 times with strains chosen at random, keeping the number of carriers removed the same. Those random drops give a median p of 0.0065, and 2 percent of them are at least as bad as dropping the cluster.
    Random drops of the same size do not hurt nearly as much, so this cluster is carrying the result rather than merely contributing to it. The finding should be read as resting on that group of relatives.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
  It settles whether averaging over the panel hid a defense system that works on part of it, which is a specific and plausible way the negative results elsewhere could have been wrong, and it is answered with the panel resolved rather than by assumption.
  It does not rescue a system whose effect is confined to phages outside this panel, nor one acting on a single phage too weakly for 402 strains to resolve, and it inherits the limitation that carriage is presence of a catalogued system rather than a functional allele.
  The matched null also removes, by construction, any effect that coincides exactly with core-genome ancestry. That is deliberate and it is the same blind spot documented for every other test here: an effect inseparable from the lineage cannot be assigned to the system by any observational design.
