INTERPRETATION
  Every flagged locus is associated with being lysed more often, not less. Nothing in the accessory genome of these 254 strains is associated with resistance once prevalence and phylogenetic confinement are matched.
  A locus associated with susceptibility is a weaker finding than it sounds. It can mark a lineage that is easy to lyse for reasons unrelated to the locus, and with carriers numbering around ten strains the estimate rests on a small group. These are observations to follow up, not mechanisms.

  One result deserves to be drawn out. This scan used no catalogue: it tested every variable k-mer in 254 genomes without reference to any annotation. Yet the strains carrying pattern 6612 also share SanaTA, Septu, which are among the systems that have shown an association with increased lysis throughout this analysis and that no defense mechanism can explain.
  Two readings fit. The locus and those systems may sit on the same mobile element, so that carrying one means carrying the other and neither causes anything. Or the element marks a group of strains that are easy to lyse for some other reason. Either way the convergence is informative about the anomaly: it is not a quirk of how defense systems were catalogued, because a scan that ignored the catalogue entirely landed on the same strains.
  It also suggests where to look. The element is present in about ten strains, is not lineage-confined, and is a few hundred bases of shared sequence in a window whose genes are listed above.
  The accompanying protein sequences are written to data/processed/gwas_locus_sequences.faa so that a reader can take them further than the profile set used here allows. The profiles searched cover anti-defense families and lysogeny machinery only, so a locus with no hit is unannotated rather than uncharacterised.
